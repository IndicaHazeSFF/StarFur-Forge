# Quiet Ember — Story Summary

**Status:** Canon through **Day 6, afternoon**, immediately after Vespera teaches the player how she evaluates freight contracts.  
**Current location:** Aboard *Quiet Ember*, en route to **Morrow’s Wake Station**.  
**Current timeline:** Morrow’s Wake is expected in roughly a day.  
**Primary POV:** Second-person present tense from the player’s perspective, with occasional reader-only Vespera POV interludes.

---

## 1. Core Premise

The player wakes aboard a small independent freighter called **Quiet Ember** with **total recent autobiographical amnesia**.

He was recovered unconscious from a failing, unregistered life-pod by the ship’s captain and sole regular crewmember, **Vespera**, a lavender-furred anthro fox.

The pod had almost no useful identifying data. Its life support was failing, the player was dehydrated and mildly hypoxic, and there were no obvious signs of catastrophic trauma. He remained unconscious aboard *Quiet Ember* for about three days before waking.

He has no confirmed name, profession, employer, home, family, friends, partner, origin vessel, destination, reason for being in the pod, or explanation for the amnesia.

His **explicit autobiographical memory remains at zero**.

However, he retains normal procedural and semantic knowledge. He knows how to speak, cook, reason, read systems, use tools, and recognize many common concepts. Over time it becomes clear that he also possesses useful technical instincts, but neither he nor Vespera treat those abilities as proof of who he used to be.

Vespera’s guiding principle is:

> **Inventory before identity.**

Knowing how to do something is not the same as remembering how he learned it.

The story therefore focuses less on forced memory flashes and more on discovering who the player is **now**, while gradually approaching the first place with enough infrastructure to search for his past: **Morrow’s Wake Station**.

---

## 2. Quiet Ember

**Quiet Ember** is a small, old, well-maintained independent freighter owned and captained by Vespera.

It is designed to be operable by one person, but that means almost every responsibility ultimately falls on Vespera: piloting, navigation, maintenance, freight selection, docking, cargo paperwork, consumables, repairs, scheduling, food, ship finances, and emergency response.

The ship is compact enough that its spaces have become familiar quickly. Important recurring spaces include the **bridge**, **galley**, **folding two-person lounge/couch**, **cargo bay**, the player’s spare cabin, Vespera’s cabin, and the shared shower/wash area.

The ship is increasingly starting to feel like somewhere the player is **returning to**, not merely somewhere he was rescued.

---

## 3. Vespera

### Basic Description

Vespera is a lavender-furred female anthro fox/vulpine with golden eyes, a slightly husky voice, one large fluffy tail, a calm controlled manner, dry humor, and strong practical instincts.

She owns and operates *Quiet Ember* independently.

### Personality

Vespera is reserved but kind, honest, practical, calm under pressure, comfortable with silence, respectful of personal space, dryly funny, naturally competent without being infallible, and accustomed to making her own decisions.

She is not secretly manipulating the player and does not have a hidden agenda.

She likes company more than she initially admits, but she also genuinely enjoys solitude. She once described loneliness as something that:

> “isn’t always bad enough to fix.”

### Background

Vespera grew up on a mid-rim station with dock-worker parents. Both parents are alive and mostly retired.

Her mother still asks whether Vespera has eaten. She drinks tea, once received an expensive imported tea set from Vespera, complained Vespera should have saved the money, then drank the tea anyway. A cabin photo shows her holding half a sandwich.

Her father still informally fixes things, pretends not to worry, and asks suspiciously detailed questions about *Quiet Ember’s* engine. He once drank half of the strongest tea blend while claiming he did not like it. When Vespera was younger, he made her clean leaf litter from a station ventilation intake after she insisted decorative plants could not cause maintenance problems.

Vespera left home young, worked aboard freighters, then eventually bought *Quiet Ember*.

### Former Partner

Vespera once had a co-pilot who was also a romantic partner. After a salvage accident, the partner survived but wanted to leave ship life and settle on a station or planet. The partner asked Vespera to come.

Vespera chose *Quiet Ember*.

The partner left at **Marrow Station**.

This is not treated as a melodramatic wound, but it helps explain why Vespera is cautious about attaching meaning too quickly to temporary situations.

### Pirate Incident

At one point in her career, Vespera was confronted by six pirates aboard one ship. She surrendered atmospheric processors, faked reactor trouble by venting coolant into secondary exchangers, trapped a boarder between airlock doors, and negotiated his release. She later admitted her hands shook for roughly two hours afterward.

She is courageous, but not invulnerable.

## 4. Vespera’s Preferences and Ordinary Life

A major story theme is the player discovering what Vespera is like when she is not simply functioning as captain.

### Food and Drink

Vespera likes strong caf, strong dark roast, a little sweetener, no cream, warm fresh bread, salted butter, fruit preserves, fresh food, and tea—especially botanical or citrus-forward tea.

Her caf became one of the player’s first recurring rituals aboard. After remembering how harsh he found her normal cup, she began quietly adjusting his with slightly more sweetener. That small act matters emotionally.

### Favorite Color

**Deep green.**

This is reinforced through her dark green blanket, coat wash, aesthetic preferences, and later her Greenwake mug.

### Media and Hobbies

She likes fair-play mysteries, logic puzzles, instrumental work music, older spacer music, films and serials, markets, quiet cruise, walking aimlessly, agricultural habitats, fresh food, and places with real plants and daylight.

For mysteries, she prefers evidence available to the reader, competent investigators, strong atmosphere, small communities or enclosed environments, characters who genuinely like one another, and darkness when earned rather than misery for its own sake.

She strongly dislikes mysteries that invent crucial evidence during the reveal.

### Green Spaces

Vespera loves agricultural and botanical habitats because she grew up around station infrastructure. She appreciates real soil, leaves, humidity, plant smells, running water, and spaces designed to feel alive rather than purely functional.

Her favorite named destination is **Calyx Reach**, a huge agricultural habitat with growing spaces, trees, daylight panels, market levels, and a bakery near the east spoke.

She once told the player:

> “If a contract ever takes us that way, I’ll show you.”

When the player said he would like that, she answered:

> “So would I.”

## 5. Vespera’s Personal Routine

Occasional reader-only POVs show Vespera’s private nightly life.

### Grooming

Vespera generally showers once per day, more if dirty from maintenance.

Her personal coat products include dark green coat wash with a citrus/leafy scent, silver coat conditioner, detangler, and a wide grooming brush.

Tail grooming involves working root to tip, checking the underside, dealing with tangles slowly, and accounting for the dense undercoat near the base. Sometimes she only does a quick pass if already groomed.

Night grooming can include neck and shoulder fur, cheek fur, ear cleaning with a damp pad, hand/foot pad checks, balm, and claw filing.

Her night drawer contains a claw file, small scissors, brush-cleaning pick, spare hair ties, pad/skin balm, basic medicine, sewing kit, and two “emergency” ration candies she never seems to eat.

### Sleep

She sleeps in an old loose shirt and soft shorts, keeps her cabin cool, uses a dark green blanket, keeps water beside the bed, and performs a final ship/status review before sleeping.

### Cabin

Vespera’s cabin is narrow but lived-in. Notable details include physical books, a tablet dock, a cup with pens and a screwdriver, a jacket, a pinboard with old docking permits, a market token, a photo of her parents, and a personal terminal.

## 6. The Player

### Core Personality

The player is curious, dorky, playful, helpful, mildly submissive in interpersonal rhythm, comfortable letting Vespera lead, still willing to suggest things or make independent choices, easily flustered when teasing is returned, capable of dry humor, genuinely interested in ordinary details, and strongly motivated by “problem exists, problem must cease existing.”

He likes being useful without wanting to become obnoxiously helpful.

He increasingly realizes he does not want every moment aboard *Quiet Ember* to be treated as a test of his missing identity. He values being able to simply exist.

### Early Behavior

When he first woke, he naturally scanned exits, noticed handholds, checked warnings, positioned himself with awareness of the room, asked before touching hazardous systems, and paid close attention to machine state.

This suggested some retained shipboard or technical familiarity, but nothing is treated as proof.

### Known Procedural Competence

He has demonstrated consistent knife work while cooking, correct handling of medical monitor tabs, vent fan diagnosis, thermal sensor troubleshooting, pressure-system tracing, compression fitting awareness, backing-wrench use, cross-thread feel, inventory coding, condition notation, structured troubleshooting, shipboard intuition, and fast systems learning.

No profession is established.

## 7. Relationship: Player and Vespera

The relationship develops slowly and naturally.

It begins as **rescuer / unidentified passenger**, becomes **trusted temporary shipmate**, then **comfortable companion**, and increasingly becomes friendship-like.

By Day 5, the player internally considers Vespera a **friend**. Vespera privately concludes that “friend” would not be an unreasonable word.

### Current Emotional State

There is trust, familiarity, comfort, light mutual attraction, and increasing emotional investment. There is no relationship label, commitment, or forced romance.

Vespera enjoys the player’s company and teasing. The player likes Vespera very much and is increasingly attached to her presence.

Both are careful not to over-interpret that while Morrow’s Wake could reveal a past life.

### Physical Comfort

Their physical comfort has grown gradually through sitting shoulder-to-shoulder on the folding lounge, shared quiet reading, relaxed proximity on the bridge, and the Greenwake tail-touch trust beat.

Vespera has told him he is “familiar,” “not difficult to have here,” and not in the way.

At one bedtime she told him:

> “I’m glad you stayed.”

### Vespera’s Private View

Vespera consciously separates attraction from entitlement. She privately believes attraction is not the problem; making attraction someone else’s problem is.

The player has not done that.

She also values that he can spend time apart without making solitude emotionally expensive, accept teasing, stop when asked, ask permission instead of assuming, and make suggestions without fighting for control.

## 8. The “Visual Inspection” Running Joke

One recurring joke began when the player was caught looking at Vespera.

In a panic he claimed:

> “Visual inspection. Routine. The captain. Preventive maintenance.”

The phrase became a running joke, with variations including “Former Senior Visual Inspector,” the department being dissolved for “political persecution,” Vespera calling it misconduct, the player trying to restore the department, and Vespera deliberately using the joke against him.

The joke is now used selectively rather than constantly.

# 10. Story Progression by Day

## Day 1 — Waking

The player wakes in the spare cabin aboard *Quiet Ember* and cannot remember his name, where he came from, or why he was in the pod.

Vespera explains the rescue calmly and does not pressure him for answers he does not have.

The player quickly begins helping with small tasks and establishes that he prefers being useful to sitting idle.

Early maintenance includes a vent fan issue, thermal sensor offset, and inspection work.

The player’s retained technical instincts become obvious.

Vespera begins the “inventory before identity” philosophy.

## Day 2 — Normalizing Aboard Ember

The player becomes more comfortable on the ship.

Additional maintenance and practical cooperation continue. The first major shared-personal beats emerge through caf, cooking, casual conversation, humor, and early visual-inspection embarrassment.

The folding lounge is deployed for shared entertainment. The player realizes Vespera likes mystery stories.

## Day 3 — Familiarity

### Maintenance

Completed: waste reclamation filter, lubrication points, and major inventory progress.

### Shared Media

The two watch a terrible mystery episode:

**CASEFILES OF THE OUTER REACH — “The Silent Maintenance Log”**

The episode cheats by introducing crucial evidence at the reveal.

The player complains:

> “This son of a bitch spawned evidence during the accusation.”

and:

> “That is downloadable content!”

Vespera is delighted to finally have someone who cares enough to complain with her.

### Driftline

Vespera teaches the player a tactical route-control board game called **Driftline**. He loses four times, then wins once.

### Bridge Quiet

Vespera dims the bridge and the two deliberately do nothing except look at stars.

Vespera admits that over years of freight work she stopped really seeing them.

The player tells her:

> “I guess I can remind you.”

This matters.

### Relationship

Vespera begins privately thinking of the player as familiar rather than purely as a rescue case.

## Day 4 — Friendship Becomes Noticeable

### Morning

Vespera makes the player caf with his preferred slightly increased sweetness. He explicitly thanks her for remembering.

The “restraint challenge” ends. Vespera finally admits:

> “You have demonstrated restraint.”

The player declares victory.

### Freight Briefing

Vespera discusses possible future loads from Morrow’s Wake:

1. Water-treatment components
2. Replacement environmental-control modules
3. Suspicious “sealed machine parts / expedited discretion”

The suspicious contract is rejected immediately.

### Cooking

The player watches Vespera cook because he is fascinated by her ordinary practiced habits: browning protein, cooking harder vegetables first, adding sauce gradually, tasting and adjusting, and using elbow/foot to close things while cooking.

The player realizes he wants to learn many ordinary things about Vespera’s life.

### Bridge Lunch

The player suggests eating lunch on the bridge with the lights dimmed and the stars visible. Vespera agrees.

This matters because the player is no longer only deferring. He is beginning to make low-pressure suggestions.

### Reading

The player reads:

**The Last Light at Harrow Pier: A Sel Marris Investigation**

It is a fair mystery. The final solution involves a maintenance worker, a beacon diagnostic camera blackout, falsified inspection certifications, and fairly planted clues.

The player withholds theories while reading to avoid being spoiled. After finishing, he and Vespera discuss the solution at length.

### Relationship

By the end of Day 4, the player internally considers Vespera a friend, Vespera privately thinks “friend” is reasonable, shared silence feels ordinary, and their dynamic feels less like rescuer/passenger and more like companions.

## Day 5 — Greenwake Conservatory

Day 5 becomes the most emotionally important day so far.

### Discovery

The player checks the passenger/nav display and independently notices **Greenwake Conservatory**, a botanical waystation built inside a hollowed asteroid.

The player recognizes that Vespera would probably love it because of her affection for green spaces.

He asks:

> “Would you want to go?”

This matters because he is not merely asking her to take him somewhere. He is asking whether **she** would enjoy it.

The detour only adds a few hours. Vespera agrees.

### Arrival

Greenwake appears externally as an unimpressive asteroid with docking structures.

Inside is a huge pressurized conservatory featuring real trees, moss, running water, walking paths, artificial daylight, birds, a market terrace, produce, food, crafts, tea, books, pottery, plants, and quiet spacer rest areas.

The player’s first reaction is:

> “Holy shit.”

### Greenwake Market

The player and Vespera wander without urgency. The player discovers unfamiliar fruit, botanical foods, Greenwake market culture, and the strange feeling of having no credits in a place full of things.

This strengthens his desire to someday earn his own money.

### Fresh Bread

The smell of fresh bread causes Vespera to stop walking and make a soft involuntary sound of pleasure.

The player jokes:

> “This seems intimate.”

and:

> “I can give you and the bread a minute.”

They buy warm hearth bread and salted butter, later adding citrus preserves.

They sit beneath an old broadleaf tree Vespera remembers from previous visits. She once spent hours reading there.

### Greenwake Conversation

Vespera admits she often delays pleasant things because there is always a practical reason not to stop. Greenwake is exactly the sort of place she can postpone for years.

Vespera later admits:

> “Whatever happens at Morrow’s Wake, I’m glad we did this first.”

The player agrees.

### Tail Permission

Later in a quiet garden area, the player finally asks:

> “Can I ask you something slightly embarrassing?”

Then:

> “Can I touch your tail?”

He does not grab. He does not hide behind the visual-inspection joke. He simply asks.

Vespera responds:

> “You finally asked.”

She agrees, setting simple boundaries: be gentle, do not pull, and do not grab near the base.

The player discovers long outer guard hairs, dense warm undercoat, much more softness than expected, and greater sensitivity nearer the base.

The moment is meaningful but not sexualized. It works because of several days of earned trust.

Vespera privately reflects that he had looked often but never presumed. He asked. That is why saying yes was easy.

### Off-Duty Vespera

The player decides not to steer the rest of the expedition. He lets Vespera simply be herself.

Vespera wanders without optimizing, changes direction, drinks tea, talks about her parents, browses books, buys a deep-green ceramic mug, considers a plant, later buys a sealed terrarium instead, watches artificial sunset, eats dinner at Greenwake, wanders the night garden, and buys a Greenwake token.

The player explicitly tells her:

> “I like seeing you like this.”

Not because Captain Vespera is bad, but because he likes seeing what she is like when she does not have to be responsible for every system and decision.

He later says:

> “I notice you.”

Vespera privately feels the difference between being observed because someone needs a decision and being noticed because someone cares.

### Artificial Sunset

Vespera explains that she originally thought artificial sunset sounded silly because it is only a lighting program.

Then she discovered that she did not care whether it was artificial.

The feeling is still real.

### Someone Else Owns the Pumps

Vespera admits that one reason Greenwake relaxes her is that if a pump breaks, someone else has to care.

She likes *Quiet Ember*, loves independence, and does not want to live on Greenwake—but she occasionally wants to be somewhere where the floor is not technically her problem.

### Night Garden

The two visit a nocturnal garden featuring bioluminescent plants, fungal glow, reflective flowers, night insects, quiet water, and artificial darkness.

Vespera admits she has not spent that long somewhere simply because she chose to in a long time.

The player emphasizes:

> “You chose it.”

He wanted the trip to be hers too.

Vespera confirms she is enjoying it and adds that she is probably enjoying it more than she would have alone.

### Return to Quiet Ember

Eventually **Vespera** decides it is time to go.

The player does not argue. He simply follows.

On returning to the ship, Vespera casually says:

> “Home.”

The player notices, but does not comment.

### Greenwake Purchases

Vespera brings aboard a deep-green ceramic mug, citrus preserves, dried citrus peel for tea, a small sealed terrarium, a Greenwake token, and the remaining bread.

The terrarium becomes a tiny living piece of Greenwake aboard *Quiet Ember*.

The new green mug replaces her old mug in the primary cupboard position.

### Evening Wind-Down

After departure, Vespera resumes course, makes citrus tea using the Greenwake peel, uses her new mug, dims the bridge, and chooses quiet star-watching.

She tells the player Greenwake was one of the best **unplanned** stops she has made in years.

She explicitly clarifies:

> “I stayed because I wanted to.”

The player is glad. He wanted Greenwake to be something Vespera genuinely enjoyed, not a trip she performed for him.

### Tail Debrief

On the bridge, Vespera later asks:

> “Was the tail actually that interesting?”

The player immediately answers:

> “Yes.”

Vespera admits she said yes because he had spent days proving that if she teased him, he would laugh; if she told him to stop, he would stop; and if he wanted something, eventually he would ask.

She also teases him for touching her tail like a museum artifact.

### Bedtime

At the cabin junction, Vespera says:

> “We should do things like that sometimes.”

Meaning: stop somewhere just because it is nice.

The player answers that he would like that.

Vespera says:

> “Me too.”

Day 5 ends.

## Day 6 — Morning

The player’s morning routine is now compressed because it is familiar.

He enters the galley. Greenwake has already become part of normal ship life. The terrarium remains alive. Vespera uses the new green mug.

Breakfast includes the remaining Greenwake bread, salted butter, and citrus preserves.

### Morrow’s Wake Briefing

Morrow’s Wake is now roughly a day away.

Vespera has already sent ahead a notice that she is arriving with a recovered unidentified survivor in stable condition with recent autobiographical amnesia.

Likely process:

1. Station approach / berth assignment
2. Customs or station control
3. Medical evaluation
4. Biometrics / record search
5. Registry / missing-person / transit record checks where available

Potential medical work includes a basic physical, neurological assessment, imaging, bloodwork, cognitive testing, and memory assessment.

Vespera reminds the player:

> “You don’t have to perform for them either.”

He can simply say what he knows and what he does not know.

### Identity Expectations

The player admits:

> “I’d like a name.”

Even if that is all he gets.

Vespera agrees that would be nice.

They establish another important principle:

> **Information is not an order.**

If Morrow’s Wake finds a name, former address, distant system of origin, contacts, or records, that information does not automatically determine what the player must do.

Vespera refuses to let a database make decisions for him.

### Temporary Credential

Vespera plans to arrange a temporary station-access credential for the player.

The player is oddly excited because it may be the first legally recognized item that is actually his.

### Inventory Complete

The ship inventory finally reaches **100% VERIFIED**.

The player is absurdly pleased and makes Vespera high-five him.

Inventory crimes have temporarily been defeated.

## Day 6 — Afternoon: Freight School

Vespera opens the freight-board cache and notices the player is interested.

She invites him to sit beside her and begins explaining how an independent freighter captain actually evaluates work.

This is new to the player, so the scene is treated in detail.

### First Rule

Vespera:

> “The number in the largest font is trying to lie to you.”

The large number is the gross payout.

She teaches that payout is not profit. Important factors include fuel, docking fees, loading/unloading costs, cargo transfer fees, insurance, declared cargo value, maintenance reserve, consumables, deadline penalties, handling requirements, cargo density, volume, stacking rules, destination infrastructure, return freight, empty repositioning, client reputation, legal risk, and shipper reliability.

### Water-Treatment Contract

Candidate: **Water-treatment components**  
Gross payout: roughly **3,280 credits**

Pros include a reasonable route, standard handling, permitted inspection, moderate deadline, good client, better chance of outbound freight after delivery, and better overall fit for *Quiet Ember*.

Vespera estimates perhaps around two thousand credits after direct trip costs, depending on conditions.

### Environmental-Control Contract

Candidate: **Replacement environmental-control modules**  
Gross payout: roughly **4,120 credits**

It looks better at first, but has a tighter deadline, worse docking costs, poor outbound freight, and a destination that mostly exports bulk mineral loads. *Quiet Ember* is not optimized for bulk ore economics.

Therefore the higher-paying contract may actually be worse.

### Suspicious Contract

Candidate: **Sealed machine parts / expedited discretion**  
Gross payout: roughly **7,900 credits**

Red flags include nearly double normal pay, accelerated pickup, vague manifest, a new intermediary account, emphasis on discretion, and restricted routine inspection.

Vespera rejects it.

Her principle:

> “I don’t have to prove a contract is criminal before declining it.”

And:

> “I just have to decide whether I want it aboard my ship.”

### Freight as a Network

The player learns that a job does not only determine income. It determines where the ship will be afterward.

This creates a network puzzle.

Vespera often thinks several legs ahead.

The player realizes a freight contract is not just cargo. It is a move on a board.

Vespera compares it to Driftline:

> “This is basically Driftline where losing costs money.”

The player immediately loves that framing.

### Player’s Interest

The player becomes genuinely interested in route chains, cargo history, station efficiency, repeated shipper behavior, destination economics, *Quiet Ember*-specific cost records, and freight pattern analysis.

Vespera notices he likes systems.

The player begins imagining how he might someday help with route comparison, recordkeeping, pattern detection, contract filtering, and reducing Vespera’s mental load.

He does **not** assume he can fix her business after one lesson.

The thought is simply:

> “I could learn this.”

### Professional Attention

The player makes a sincere effort to focus professionally on the freight board.

Mostly succeeds.

There are occasional tiny lapses where he notices Vespera herself.

Vespera notices that too.

Neither turns it into a full flirting scene.

### Why Vespera Has Not Accepted a Contract Yet

Vespera currently prefers the water-treatment job.

But she has not accepted it.

Reason: Morrow’s Wake may identify the player and reveal something that changes where he needs or wants to go.

She tells him:

> “I don’t know what tomorrow changes.”

She is keeping *Quiet Ember* flexible.

When the player asks if that is “for me,” Vespera answers:

> “For the situation.”

Then:

> “Which includes you.”

The player thanks her.

# 13. Morrow’s Wake

**Morrow’s Wake Station** is the next major destination.

It is a small independent station built around an older mineral-processing hub.

Likely facilities include docking, medical, customs, a modest residential population, better data access, freight board, resupply, maintenance, and fuel.

### Purpose

The player will undergo medical evaluation, identity search, biometrics, registry checks, missing-person checks, and transit-record checks where available.

### Desired Identity Reveal

The story should provide **limited but meaningful identity information**.

At minimum: a usable name.

Possibly: a very distant system of origin or a few non-actionable record fragments.

Do **not** immediately reveal the full family, full life story, profession, complete mystery solution, convenient parent vessel, or full biography.

The player’s identity should remain partially unresolved.

# 17. Important Story Rules Going Forward

## Pacing

### Compress

- Repeated maintenance
- Familiar ship routines
- Routine meals
- Repeated navigation checks
- Familiar corridors
- Ordinary transit
- Repeated inventory / housekeeping

### Do Not Compress

- Anything **new to the player**
- First-time docking/undocking/station procedures
- New technology
- New environments
- New cultural experiences
- New ship systems
- New bureaucracy
- New work types
- Personal moments
- Relationship developments
- Vespera POVs
- Consequential discoveries

### Rule of Thumb

**Repeated routine = compress.**

Continue occasional brief reader-only Vespera POV interludes.

Do not use Vespera POV to reveal hidden future canon.

# 21. Overall Tone

The story is built around calm sci-fi, ordinary shipboard life, dry humor, improvised canon, slow trust, competence, mundane intimacy, friendship before romance, quiet attraction, new experiences, technical curiosity, shared routines, and occasional emotional weight without melodrama.

The most important recurring idea is that the player’s future should not be determined entirely by whatever his forgotten past turns out to be.

Morrow’s Wake may provide information.

But:

> **Information is not an order.**
