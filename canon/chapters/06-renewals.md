# Chapter 6 — Renewals

## Renewals

The contract clinic was quiet.
Rows of simple seating lined the wall outside the administration terminals. Several mercenaries sat waiting in the chairs, their postures relaxed in the way of people accustomed to long deployments and longer bureaucratic procedures.
Most of them were not human.
A scaled reptilian figure leaned back in one chair scrolling through a contract display projected above their wrist. Two lupine mercenaries spoke quietly near the far wall, their ears turning occasionally toward the overhead announcement speakers.
No one seemed impatient.
Renewal days were routine.
Murr stood near one of the open terminals, reading the contract screen with careful attention.
The agreement text scrolled slowly down the display.
Operational Contract Renewal — Active Asset Status
Several paragraphs of legal language followed.
Most of it concerned arbitration participation requirements, operational availability windows, and standard performance obligations.
A smaller section addressed metabolic stabilization.
Murr read that part twice.
Failure to maintain authorized Sugar supplementation may result in severe physiological destabilization.
She considered the statement.
If I did not renew, she thought,
I would not be able to visit the restaurant again. That would be… unfortunate.
She pressed ACCEPT.
The terminal chimed softly.
CONTRACT RENEWED
Assignment data began loading automatically beneath the confirmation message.
Before reading it, Murr glanced briefly at the medical station across the room.
A technician waved her forward.
“Meter refill?”
“Yes,” Murr said.
She crossed the room and sat in the examination chair.
The technician worked efficiently, opening a small access panel along the side of Murr’s metering device where it rested against the fur along her ribs. The unit was compact and smooth, its surface broken only by a small diagnostic display and several sealed injection ports.
The technician removed an empty cartridge and replaced it with a fresh one.
The motion was practiced and quick.
“Baseline reservoir,” the technician said, checking the readout. “You’re running low.”
“That is why I am here,” Murr replied.
The technician smiled faintly and sealed the device again.
A quiet click followed as the pump synchronized with the new cartridge.
Warmth spread slowly through Murr’s bloodstream as the system recalibrated.
Her breathing settled into a steadier rhythm.
The technician glanced at the monitor once more.
“Stable,” they said. “You’re good to go.”
Murr nodded and returned to the terminal.
The new assignment data had finished loading.
ARBITRATION NOTICE
Location data scrolled across the screen.
Another corporate dispute.
Another arbitration window.
Murr accepted the briefing packet without hesitation.
As she stepped away from the terminal, a small thought surfaced quietly in the back of her mind:
If I don’t renew…

The surge began exactly on schedule.
Operator Hale felt it first as a tightening behind the ribs as the metering device along his flank increased output. Neural response accelerated. Peripheral awareness sharpened until every motion across the skyline registered instantly.
The city below should have been empty.
It wasn’t.
Traffic moved through the streets as though nothing unusual was happening. A delivery drone passed between buildings. Lights flickered on inside apartment windows.
Hale’s jaw tightened.
“Clearance still pending,” someone muttered across the team channel.
The surge continued building.
His muscles drew tight with anticipation, every system leaning toward the moment when CANON would authorize engagement.
It felt like holding his breath before a sprint.
Seconds stretched.
No authorization.
Across the rooftop another operator shifted their stance, the small movement sharp with restrained irritation.
“This is wrong,” someone said quietly.
The surge plateaued.
Energy sat in Hale’s bloodstream with nowhere to go.
Then the channel opened.
Elias’s voice came through, calm and precise.
“Hold position.”
A brief pause.
“Await confirmation.”
Hale exhaled slowly through his nose and forced his shoulders to relax while the metering device continued feeding the surge through his system.
They waited.
Thirty seconds passed.
Then sixty.
The city continued moving beneath them.
When the recall order finally arrived it sounded almost routine.
“Stand down,” Elias said.
The metering device along Hale’s side began throttling output back toward baseline. The tension in his muscles drained slowly, leaving behind the hollow sensation of a motion that had never happened.
Hale rolled one shoulder once.
The team began breaking position.
—
Three levels below the street, inside a service corridor beneath the target structure, the same surge built inside another team.
Operator Imani felt the heat of it spreading through her muscles as the metering device along her ribs primed the engagement dose.
Her ears twitched slightly beneath the edge of her helmet.
Everything was ready.
Above them, footsteps crossed the sidewalk.
Civilian.
Too many.
Imani frowned.
“This should be clear,” someone whispered across the channel.
Another operator flexed their fingers once against the grip of their equipment.
The surge climbed higher.
Like holding a sneeze that refused to happen.
The team waited.
Then Maeve’s voice cut cleanly across the channel.
“Hold.”
Calm. Controlled.
“Await confirmation.”
The corridor stilled immediately.
No one moved.
Imani could feel the surge sitting in her bloodstream now, energy coiled tight against the moment of release.
Above them a car door slammed.
Still no authorization.
Another long pause.
Then Maeve spoke again.
“Stand down.”
A quiet breath moved through the team.
The metering devices began bleeding the surge away as systems returned to baseline output.
“Return to staging,” Maeve added.
Imani flexed her fingers once before turning toward the tunnel exit.
The moment had passed.
Again.
