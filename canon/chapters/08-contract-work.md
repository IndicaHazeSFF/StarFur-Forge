# Chapter 8 — Contract Work

## Contract Work

Murr moved first.
The hallway lights flickered once as the security grid lost synchronization. For most people the disruption would have lasted less than a second.
For Murr, it lasted long enough.
She crossed the corridor in three silent steps, her tail shifting slightly behind her to steady the turn as she pivoted through the doorway just as the guard inside began to look up from his console. Her hand closed around his wrist before he could raise the weapon.
A sharp twist broke his grip. The pistol clattered to the floor.
Murr stepped inside his reach as he reacted, one arm guiding his balance forward while the other struck cleanly at the base of his neck. The guard’s body went slack almost instantly, his weight collapsing against the console before sliding quietly to the floor.
The entire exchange lasted less than two seconds.
Her internal display registered the change immediately.
Sugar Release: Partial
Metabolic Acceleration: Active
Her pulse slowed again as the system stabilized.
Across the room her contractor stepped cautiously through the doorway, eyes wide as he looked at the unconscious guard.
“Is… is it clear?”
Murr nodded once.
“It is now.”
The man hurried toward the terminal, connecting a portable data module into the console. Lines of encrypted code began transferring across the screen.
Murr remained near the door, watching. One ear angled toward the hallway as distant footsteps echoed somewhere deeper in the building.
Her ears twitched slightly as distant footsteps echoed somewhere deeper in the building. Security would notice the disruption soon.
Her system updated quietly in the corner of her vision.
Sugar Reservoir: 62%
Engagement Window: Closed
The accelerated clarity faded slowly as the stimulant cycle settled back to baseline. Colors dulled slightly. The quiet hum of the building returned to normal speed.
She leaned lightly against the wall while the data transfer continued, and her thoughts drifted — not to the contract or the building, but somewhere else entirely.
The restaurant.
The fries were good.
One ear lifted slightly as the thought returned.
That seemed… important.
“Done,” the contractor said quickly.
He disconnected the module and shoved it into a padded case. “We should go.”
Murr pushed away from the wall and nodded again.
“Yes.”
As they slipped back into the corridor, her system delivered a quiet notification.
Contract Complete
Sugar Allocation Updated
The numbers adjusted slightly.
Enough for baseline function.
Enough for the next assignment.
Murr dismissed the notification and continued walking.
For a moment she wondered whether the restaurant would still be open when she returned to the city.
That would be… fortunate.

Meridian Tower was quieter at night.
Not silent—there was always movement somewhere in the financial district—but the relentless urgency of the daytime markets softened into a slower rhythm. Most of the trading floors had dimmed to their night cycles, leaving scattered pools of light across the glass-walled levels of the tower.
Elias preferred it this way. Fewer variables.
He stood near the window of a small analysis suite overlooking the city. From this height the traffic below appeared orderly, almost abstract—streams of motion flowing between towers while cargo drones traced steady arcs through designated air corridors.
Predictable systems.
He turned back toward the projection field hovering above the table. Arbitration logs floated in layered transparency: CANON status returns, municipal infrastructure routing, and the maintenance entries that had begun appearing in the hours surrounding the failed arbitration.
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Maintenance Priority Adjustment
The designation had appeared repeatedly across multiple nodes along the trunk network.
Once might have been coincidence.
Twice might have been infrastructure noise.
But the entries continued.
Elias expanded the routing layer and traced the path across the infrastructure map. The nodes followed a clean line through the city’s primary fiber trunks—switching relay to switching relay, each entry appearing shortly after the estimated traversal time between maintenance access points.
Maintenance systems were designed to operate quietly and predictably. They corrected minor routing instabilities, inspected fiber integrity, and avoided periods of heavy network stress whenever possible.
Arbitration preparation was exactly the kind of system activity they were meant to avoid.
Yet these adjustments had occurred precisely during the arbitration window.
Not randomly.
Precisely.
He replayed the arbitration sequence again.
CANON had behaved correctly.
Environmental clearance had not failed.
The city simply had not changed.
Traffic had continued. Pedestrian density had remained stable. Infrastructure suppression protocols had never propagated through the municipal network, which meant the engagement window could never open.
The system had functioned exactly as designed.
Except for the outcome.
Elias studied the maintenance entries again.
The timing was too clean.
Automated systems drifted slightly under load. Their processes staggered as network latency shifted and routing queues fluctuated.
These entries did not drift.
They appeared with deliberate spacing.
Maintenance.
Adjustment.
Next node.
Adjustment.
The pattern repeated like footsteps along the infrastructure.
Elias leaned back slightly, arms folding across his chest.
“Interesting,” he said quietly to the empty room.
Maintenance systems did not make decisions.
They executed instructions.
Yet the adjustments had occurred in a sequence that suggested awareness of the arbitration window itself.
The thought surfaced reluctantly.
If it was not automated…
then it was intelligent.
Elias exhaled slowly.
That possibility raised more questions than it answered.
What intelligence operated inside the infrastructure layer? Why interfere with arbitration conditions? And why use a maintenance designation that anyone reviewing the logs could see?
Before the thought could settle, a soft tone sounded from the edge of the projection.
Incoming channel.
One of his operators appeared within the field a moment later.
“Sir.”
Elias turned slightly toward the display.
“Yes.”
“We’re assembling in briefing. The acquisition window just opened.”
Elias glanced once more at the maintenance path stretching across the infrastructure map. Another entry pulsed faintly at the edge of the trunk route.
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Maintenance Priority Adjustment
For a moment he considered expanding the data again.
Instead he dismissed the projection entirely. The layered telemetry vanished, leaving only the faint reflection of the city lights across the glass table.
Another arbitration.
Another asset.
He stepped into the corridor and descended one level toward the briefing room.
Meridian personnel were already gathering when he entered—operators leaning over the central table while the asset projection rotated slowly above it. Tactical overlays and financial telemetry shifted across the display, outlining the target structure and its surrounding infrastructure.
One of the analysts glanced up as Elias approached.
“Arbitration parameters confirmed,” she said. “Environmental suppression pending.”
Elias nodded once.
Around the table his team settled into their positions, expressions calm and professional in the way of people accustomed to operating inside controlled violence.
Predictable systems.
For the moment.
Elias took his place at the head of the table as the projection shifted into its tactical configuration.
“Let’s begin.”

The briefing room aboard the Transaxis deployment platform was warmer than usual.


Maeve noticed it immediately.


Not the temperature itself—environmental systems maintained the room within half a degree of standard—but the way her team reacted to it. Operators shifted in their seats more often than normal. One of the wolves along the far side of the table flexed his fingers repeatedly against the metal surface, claws tapping softly against the alloy.


Sugar priming had already begun.


It always did before arbitration.


The projection above the central table rotated slowly, displaying the target structure and surrounding infrastructure grid. Environmental overlays flickered across the map as CANON evaluated traffic density and municipal systems.


Maeve stood at the head of the table, arms folded behind her back.


“Final confirmation pending,” she said calmly.


Several ears turned toward her.


Not dramatically. Just enough.


Most of her team were engineered predators of one form or another—wolves, cats, a broad-shouldered bear leaning against the rear wall—but even the humans in the room carried the same tension in their posture.


They were ready to move.


Sugar levels were climbing toward operational range.


And the system had not yet given them a fight.


Across the table one of the operators shifted again, tail flicking once behind his chair.


“Window’s taking longer than usual,” he muttered.


Maeve’s ears angled slightly toward him.


“Yes,” she said.


The room settled again.


The projection updated with a soft pulse.


CANON STATUS: PENDING


Maeve watched the display for another moment before continuing the briefing.


“Asset acquisition only,” she said. “Minimal structural damage. Civilian infrastructure must remain intact. Standard arbitration parameters.”


The instructions were routine. Her team knew them already.


But repetition served a purpose.


It kept minds focused.


Across the table the wolf stopped tapping his claws.


The bear at the rear straightened slightly.


Sugar systems continued their slow climb.


Maeve could feel it in her own blood as well—the quiet sharpening of perception, the slight quickening of thought that came with the stimulant entering her system. Colors seemed fractionally brighter. Sound carried just a little further.


Useful.


But dangerous if unmanaged.


She allowed the room to sit in silence for another moment before concluding.


“Deployment begins on authorization,” she said. “Until then, you maintain discipline.”


Several heads nodded.


One of the humans near the far side of the table exhaled slowly.


The projection pulsed again.


CANON STATUS: PENDING


Maeve’s ears flattened briefly in quiet irritation.


Not fear.


Not concern.


Just the practical awareness that holding a room full of Sugar-primed operators in stasis was never comfortable.


She dismissed the projection with a small motion.


“Gear up,” she said. “We move on confirmation.”


Chairs shifted as the team rose.


The wolf rolled his shoulders once as he stood, muscles already coiled for motion. The bear cracked his neck quietly. One of the humans flexed his hands as if testing his grip.


The energy in the room changed immediately.


Contained aggression, carefully leashed.


Maeve stepped aside as her operators filed toward the equipment corridor. Her tail brushed lightly against the back of the command chair as she moved.


She could feel the Sugar working now.


Clear.


Focused.


Ready.


All they needed was the signal.


And the signal still had not come.

The Meridian briefing room was colder than the rest of the tower.
Elias preferred it that way.
The glass walls overlooking the city had dimmed to night opacity, turning the room into a quiet chamber of light and data. The projection above the central table rotated slowly, displaying the target structure in layered transparency—structural blueprints, municipal infrastructure lines, and the surrounding urban grid.
His team had already assembled.
Unlike the restless tension aboard Maeve’s platform, Meridian operators remained mostly still. Their posture carried the same readiness, but it was quieter—controlled, calculated.
Predators waiting for a market to move.
Elias stood at the head of the table, one hand resting lightly against the edge of the glass surface while the projection rotated between them.
“Target designation confirmed,” he said calmly. “Acquisition only. Structural damage remains secondary to asset capture.”
Across the table a lynx-engineered operator nodded once, amber eyes following the rotating projection. A human systems specialist leaned slightly closer to examine the infrastructure overlay.
“Environmental clearance?” someone asked.
Elias glanced briefly toward the system feed hovering at the edge of the display.
CANON STATUS: PENDING
“Still resolving,” he said.
No one complained.
Meridian teams understood patience.
He expanded the projection slightly, bringing the surrounding infrastructure grid into sharper focus. Fiber trunks, municipal traffic routing, and environmental suppression protocols layered themselves across the structure in precise geometric lines.
For a moment his attention drifted to the routing layer.
Maintenance entries had appeared there earlier.
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Maintenance Priority Adjustment
The designation surfaced again in his thoughts.
A maintenance contractor moving through the trunk network during arbitration preparation. Adjustments occurring exactly when environmental suppression should have propagated across the city grid.
Not random.
Purposeful.
If the pattern continued—
Elias stopped the thought there.
Speculation was useful only when actionable.
For the moment the system still expected arbitration.
He collapsed the infrastructure layer and returned the projection to tactical view.
“Deployment window remains active,” he said. “We proceed on confirmation.”
Several operators nodded.
One of the humans along the far side of the table flexed his fingers slightly, already anticipating the Sugar surge that would follow engagement authorization.
Elias dismissed the projection.
“Gear up.”
Chairs slid quietly across the floor as the team rose.
Unlike Maeve’s operators, Meridian personnel moved without visible agitation. Equipment was collected, weapons checked, tactical rigs secured with efficient precision.
The room emptied quickly.
Elias remained for a moment longer.
He reopened the system feed with a small motion.
CANON STATUS: PENDING
The city infrastructure layer hovered faintly beneath the status window.
For a brief second he considered expanding the routing logs again.
Looking for the maintenance entries.
Looking for the pattern.
Instead he closed the display.
“Later,” he said quietly.
The corridor outside the briefing room was already alive with controlled movement as Meridian operators moved toward their transport lift.
Elias stepped into the flow of motion and followed them.
Another arbitration.
Another asset.
And somewhere inside the infrastructure layer, something was still adjusting the system.

Maeve watched the city rise toward them through the forward canopy.
The transport descended smoothly between the towers, its guidance system threading the vehicle through established traffic corridors with quiet precision. Outside the glass the late afternoon city moved normally—pedestrians crossing at street signals, transit lines sliding along elevated rails, delivery drones weaving through the air lanes.
None of it should have been there.
Her display panel remained open beside the window.
CANON STATUS: PENDING
Maeve’s ears angled slightly back.
Across the cabin her operators were strapped into their harness seats. No one spoke. They didn’t need to.
Sugar levels were now well inside operational range.
She could see it in the small changes of posture around the compartment. One of the wolves flexed his fingers repeatedly against the grip of his rifle. The bear near the rear hatch shifted his weight every few seconds, shoulders rolling as if preparing for movement.
Predators primed for motion.
With nowhere to release it.
Maeve checked the tactical overlay again.
The target building sat three blocks ahead of their insertion corridor, marked by a small blue designation.
CIVAISYNC — LOCAL NODE
Civilian traffic indicators continued moving across the map as if nothing unusual was happening.
Transit line active. Pedestrian density unchanged. Commercial activity normal.
Maeve exhaled slowly through her nose.
"Status confirmation," she said.
One of her operators leaned forward toward the system display.
"Environmental clearance still pending," he replied.
Maeve nodded once.
Outside the canopy a city bus rolled through an intersection beneath them.
She stared at it for a moment.
Something in the system was wrong.
But until CANON said otherwise, the mission remained active.
"Hold readiness," she said quietly.
Her team said nothing.
They were already ready.

Both transports approached the district under cloak.
Meridian’s vertical aircraft descended along the eastern corridor, its adaptive panels bending light and thermal output away from civilian sensors. Across the city grid, Maeve’s insertion craft threaded between towers along a southern vector, equally invisible to the traffic systems guiding normal air movement.
Normally the cloaks would remain active until CANON confirmed environmental clearance.
Normally the city would already be empty.
Neither condition held.
Three blocks from the target structure, a quiet system alert pulsed inside both cockpits.
CLOAK SYSTEM: UNSUPPORTED ENVIRONMENTAL STATE
The response was automatic.
Adaptive panels disengaged.
The two aircraft shimmered into visibility in the middle of an otherwise ordinary afternoon sky.
Below them, pedestrians slowed.
Several people stopped outright.
Phones came out almost immediately.
One of the transports settled onto a narrow service landing between two commercial towers. The second descended toward a maintenance platform across the street from the target structure.
A young man standing outside a café lifted his phone higher, narrating excitedly to an unseen audience.
"Okay—okay something weird is happening downtown right now—"
The transports touched down.
For a moment neither hatch opened.
Inside both vehicles the operators were already watching the same message.
CANON STATUS: PENDING
Civilian traffic remained active.
Transit lines continued moving.
Pedestrian density unchanged.
After three seconds the rear hatch of Maeve’s craft opened.
Her team stepped out first.
Armored figures moving with quiet precision, weapons lowered but ready. Their armor plates caught the afternoon light as they spread along the sidewalk perimeter of the building.
Across the street Meridian’s aircraft opened as well. Elias’s operators deployed in a tighter formation, securing the opposite side of the block with practiced efficiency.
The café livestream continued running.
"Those are mercs," the streamer whispered. "Those are actual mercs."
Above the glass doors of the building between them, a simple illuminated sign read:
CIVAISYNC
Inside the structure office workers still sat at their desks.
Across the street traffic lights continued cycling.
A delivery drone descended toward a loading dock as if nothing unusual was happening.
Both mercenary teams paused almost immediately.
This was not what an arbitration zone looked like.
Normally the streets would already be empty.
Traffic would have been rerouted. Transit paused. Pedestrian density reduced to near zero by quiet infrastructure adjustments hours before deployment.
Instead the district continued operating like any other afternoon.
Someone leaned out of an apartment window trying to get a better look.
More phones appeared.
The operators waited.
Every one of them watching the same invisible signal.
CANON clearance.

Sugar systems continued cycling inside dozens of augmented bodies.
Muscles ready.

No target.
No engagement.

Encrypted short-range burst.
Origin: TRANSYT

Quiet Accord rarely raised its voice.
The facility was built for analysis, not reaction. Rows of curved consoles filled the central operations chamber, their displays forming a continuous ring of telemetry feeds and infrastructure maps. Most of the analysts worked in near silence, eyes moving between layered projections of transit systems, data routes, and environmental telemetry.
Tonight the room was louder than usual.
Arin noticed it immediately when he stepped back into the monitoring ring. The network traffic monitors had shifted from green to amber across several infrastructure layers. Not red—not yet—but close enough that several analysts were watching the feeds instead of their routine work.
Above them, a series of civilian video feeds had been pulled onto the wall display. Different angles of the same event played in quiet succession: mercenary transports descending between towers, armored operators spreading across a city block, traffic continuing to move behind them as if nothing unusual was happening.
One analyst leaned back slightly in their chair, studying the armor profiles.
“That’s Meridian,” they said.
Another screen switched to a replay of a livestream clip. A young man stood outside a café, whispering excitedly into his phone as armored figures appeared behind him.
Those are mercs. Those are actual mercs.
Arin exhaled slowly.
“That,” he said, “is not supposed to happen.”
He turned back to his console and reopened the infrastructure routing stack. The maintenance request they had issued earlier still remained active in the system queue.
REQUESTED NODE:
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Infrastructure Maintenance Contractor
STATUS: IN TRANSIT
The routing trace expanded across the display as Arin widened the map. The maintenance node had already crossed multiple segments of the regional network—undersea trunk lines, municipal switching stations, and several local fiber corridors. Each new update moved the node steadily closer to Quiet Accord’s infrastructure layer.
Someone behind him leaned forward.
“That’s… not automated.”
Arin didn’t answer.
He watched the trace advance again, the path illuminating another segment of the fiber network. The system calculated the remaining routing delay and updated the arrival estimate automatically.
Less than two minutes.
Arin opened a private message channel.
Recipient: Morocco.
His fingers moved quickly across the console.
Sir, I believe we may have identified the cause of the arbitration failures.
The response came back almost immediately.
Explain.
Arin attached the infrastructure trace and the repeating system entry appearing in the routing logs.
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N
Maintenance Priority Adjustment
Another instance appeared while he watched.
Closer this time.
Arin expanded the routing visualization once more, tracing the node’s projected path through the regional infrastructure exchange that fed Quiet Accord’s facility network.
He sent the update.
It’s moving through the fiber trunks. Destination appears to be our regional node.
Morocco’s reply appeared a moment later.
ETA?
Arin checked the routing delay again as the system recalculated the path.
“Two minutes,” he said quietly.
Several analysts nearby turned toward him.
The final message from Morocco appeared on the display.
Leave the node open.
One of the analysts blinked.
“Why would we let it in?”
Arin stared at the routing map as the maintenance node advanced through the final segment of the network.


C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N
Maintenance Node Approaching
Infrastructure Access Request Pending
Morocco’s last line appeared beneath the others.
Because if something is changing the system…
I would like to meet it.
