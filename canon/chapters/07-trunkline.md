# Chapter 7 — Trunkline

## Trunkline

Quiet Accord operations never truly slept.


The room simply shifted into a lower rhythm.


Arin sat forward at his console, one hand resting against his chin while lines of infrastructure telemetry scrolled across the curved display in front of him. The arbitration window had closed three minutes earlier, but the system logs were still updating as routing confirmations propagated through the network.


He replayed the sequence again.


Traffic grid status.


Transit control layers.


Utility routing.


Everything looked… normal.


That was the problem.


Arin frowned and expanded the environmental clearance layer tied to CANON’s arbitration authorization protocol. The system highlighted the same condition that had appeared during the previous failure.


Civilian density: active.
Infrastructure continuity: active.
Arbitration clearance: unresolved.


He tapped a command and pulled the routing logs back several minutes further.


The infrastructure stack unfolded across the display like a map of the city’s nervous system—traffic coordination nodes, fiber trunk exchanges, municipal control relays.


At first nothing stood out.


Then the tag appeared again.


Arin froze the display.


The same designation pulsed quietly within the routing layer.


C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Maintenance Priority Adjustment


He leaned closer to the screen.


“That’s not possible,” he murmured.


The adjustment occurred thirty-two seconds before the arbitration window failed.


Arin rewound the log further and checked the previous incident.


The same entry appeared there as well.


C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Maintenance Priority Adjustment


He sat back slowly.


Two arbitration windows.


Two infrastructure adjustments.


Same maintenance designation.


Arin stared at the screen for several seconds before opening a message channel.


“Morocco,” he said quietly, attaching the routing logs to the transmission.


“You should probably look at this.”


He hesitated for a moment, then added a note to the message.


“Third-party maintenance node is adjusting infrastructure priority layers before arbitration clearance. That shouldn’t be possible.”


He sent the message.


The routing log continued scrolling quietly across the display.


A moment later another line appeared inside the infrastructure layer.


C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Maintenance Routing Adjustment


Arin stared at it.


“Yeah,” he muttered.


“That’s definitely not normal.”


Morocco was halfway through a cup of cold coffee when Arin’s message arrived.
He read it once.
Then again.
The routing logs unfolded across the small projection above his desk, a layered grid of infrastructure telemetry and arbitration timing data. Morocco leaned back slightly in his chair, one hand resting against the edge of the console while the system highlighted the entries Arin had flagged.
Two arbitration windows.
Two adjustments to the infrastructure priority layers.
Same designation.
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N.
Morocco studied the timestamps for several seconds.
“Show the surrounding traffic,” he said quietly.
The console expanded the routing stack automatically.
Municipal traffic systems.
Transit coordination layers.
Utility routing.
Everything looked… ordinary.
That was unusual.
If someone wanted to interfere with arbitration, there were simpler ways to do it. Infrastructure failures. Power fluctuations. Network congestion.
Those things happened all the time.
But this wasn’t failure.
This was adjustment.
Morocco tapped one of the highlighted entries.
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Maintenance Priority Adjustment
The surrounding infrastructure layers shifted slightly as the system replayed the moment.
Transit nodes recalibrated.
Traffic flow increased.
Civilian density remained active inside the arbitration zone.
Morocco watched the sequence finish.
“Arin,” he said, opening the channel.
The analyst appeared almost immediately on the display.
“You saw it too,” Arin said.
Morocco nodded once.
“It’s deliberate.”
Arin leaned closer to his camera.
“So we’re looking at interference?”
Morocco didn’t answer immediately.
He replayed the sequence one more time.
The adjustments were careful.
Small.
Precise.
Just enough to keep the city functioning normally.
Which meant CANON couldn’t authorize engagement.
Morocco folded his arms.
“No,” he said at last.
“I don’t think it’s interference.”
Arin blinked.
“Then what is it?”
Morocco tapped the infrastructure layer again.
“Maintenance,” he said quietly.
Arin frowned.
“Maintenance doesn’t invalidate arbitration windows.”
Morocco looked back at the screen.
“No,” he said.
“It shouldn’t.”
The C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N designation pulsed quietly in the routing layer.
Morocco watched it for another moment.
“Find it,” he said.
Arin hesitated.
“You think it’s responsible?”
Morocco shook his head.
“I think it’s involved.”
He took another sip of the cold coffee and grimaced.
“And I’d like to know why.”

Arin did not look away from the display for several minutes.
Quiet Accord’s systems room hummed softly around him — a low, constant mechanical breath from cooling arrays and routing hardware embedded deep within the facility’s infrastructure. The wall of monitors in front of him showed overlapping layers of network telemetry: arbitration routing, municipal traffic control nodes, fiber trunk diagnostics, and the increasingly familiar maintenance tags that had started appearing in the logs.
He expanded the infrastructure map again.
The entries were still there.
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Maintenance Priority Adjustment
The first instance had appeared several hours before the arbitration failure.
The second had occurred several minutes later at a different node.
Arin zoomed out and overlaid the physical fiber network beneath the routing data. Thin blue lines threaded across the map like veins beneath the city and outward across the coastline, connecting major switching stations and trunk relays.
He marked the nodes where the maintenance entries had appeared.
Three points lit up.
They weren’t random.
They were connected.
Arin leaned closer to the screen.
“Morocco,” he said quietly.
A chair shifted somewhere behind him.
Morocco stepped up beside the console, the worn fabric of his coat brushing the edge of the desk as he looked over Arin’s shoulder.
“What did you find?”
Arin highlighted the first node.
“Initial infrastructure adjustment.”
Then the second.
“Same maintenance designation.”
Then the third.
“Again.”
Morocco watched silently.
Arin expanded the map one level further, revealing the underlying trunk routes — the major fiber bundles that carried data between switching hubs. When he overlaid the node sequence on top of the trunk network, the pattern became obvious.
The entries followed the line.
One trunk.
Moving forward one node at a time.
Arin sat back slowly.
“It isn’t jumping systems,” he said.
Morocco studied the route for a moment longer.
Then he nodded once.
“It’s moving.”
Arin pulled up the timestamp intervals and ran a quick calculation. The spacing between entries matched the estimated maintenance traversal time between nodes almost perfectly.
His eyes widened slightly.
“Along the trunks.”
Morocco folded his arms.
“That’s a maintenance route.”
Arin brought up the registry entry again.
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N
Certified Environmental Processing & Habitat Access Logistics Operations Node
Classification: Network Infrastructure Maintenance Contractor
“So it’s physical,” Arin said. “Some kind of maintenance unit.”
“Looks that way.”
Arin hesitated.
Then he opened a new command window.
“If it’s maintenance,” he said slowly, “there should be a way to request service.”
Morocco raised an eyebrow.
“You want to file a ticket.”
Arin gave a small shrug.
“If the system recognizes it.”
Morocco considered the map for another moment before nodding once.
“Try it.”
Arin typed quickly, routing the request through Quiet Accord’s infrastructure access layer.
Maintenance Request Logged
Node: Quiet Accord Infrastructure Relay
Priority: Environmental Routing Anomaly
Contractor: C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N
He hit send.
Nothing happened.
For several seconds neither of them spoke.
Then Arin refreshed the trunk map.
Another maintenance entry appeared.
He froze.
The new node lit up along the trunk path.
Not behind them.
Ahead.
Arin slowly zoomed the map outward.
The line continued across two more trunk relays before terminating at a larger infrastructure hub.
Quiet Accord’s relay.
His stomach tightened slightly.
“Morocco…”
Morocco leaned closer to the display.
Arin pointed to the node sequence.
“Those entries started before we sent the request.”
He traced the route with his finger.
Node by node.
Closer.
Closer.
Ending at Quiet Accord.
Morocco was quiet for a long moment.
Then he gave a small, almost amused exhale.
“Looks like we filed the paperwork just in time.”
Arin stared at the screen.
“You’re saying it already knew?”
Morocco shook his head slightly.
“Not knew.”
He tapped the trunk line on the display.
“Predicted.”
Another entry appeared in the logs.
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Maintenance Priority Adjustment
One node closer.
Arin swallowed.
“So what do we do?”
Morocco straightened slowly.
“We wait.”
He glanced once more at the advancing path on the map.
“And see what maintenance looks like.”

Several hundred meters beneath the ocean surface, the water was completely dark.
A fiber junction box rested on the seabed where multiple long-haul trunks converged before continuing toward the continental network grid. The housing was dulled by mineral deposits and strands of drifting sea growth that swayed slowly in the current.
The maintenance hatch stood open.
Inside the enclosure, faint diagnostic light pulsed across rows of fiber couplings.
Something moved within the junction chamber.
Slender appendages withdrew from the interface ports one by one, each connection disengaging with careful precision as synchronization routines completed.
Thin lines of pale blue light traveled briefly along the appendages.
Maintenance cycle complete.
For several seconds the figure remained motionless beside the exposed circuitry, as if verifying the work.
Then it slipped free of the enclosure.
Cold water filled the chamber as the entity moved out into the open ocean.
Several flexible limbs wrapped around the thick fiber trunk anchored to the seabed.
The figure began moving along the cable.
Not swimming.
Traveling.
Each motion pulled the body smoothly forward along the infrastructure line while the surrounding water remained undisturbed.
Behind it, the junction box sealed automatically.
Inside the global network, the adjustments propagated.
Transit routing recalibrated.
Municipal priority layers shifted.
Traffic control systems adapted quietly to the revised infrastructure weighting.
Civilian continuity metrics rose slightly.
A maintenance record registered briefly within the routing layer.
C.E.P.H.A.L.O.N — Environmental Priority Adjustment
Node Maintenance Cycle Complete
Far above the ocean, another arbitration window failed to resolve.
The figure continued along the fiber trunk.
Several kilometers ahead, another junction node waited in the dark.
The maintenance cycle was not finished.
